Unit Pricing: The 10-Second Habit That Cuts Your Grocery Bill

How to read unit prices, beat shrinkflation and spot fake 'value packs' — with real-world comparison tips.

By 89944 Price Lab Editorial · Updated November 2026

On this page
  1. How to read a shelf label
  2. Three value myths
  3. Shrinkflation
  4. Build the habit

Supermarkets are designed for fast decisions. Package sizes vary, multi-buy offers look generous, and the "family size" label implies savings. The one number that cuts through all of it is the unit price: the cost per 100 grams, per litre or per item.

How to read a shelf label

Many stores print the unit price in small type on the shelf tag. Make sure you are comparing the same unit: some labels show per 100 g and others per kg, or per ounce versus per pound. When in doubt, use the calculator to normalise them.

Three value myths

  1. "Bigger is always cheaper." Often true at regular prices — but a promotion on a smaller pack frequently beats the bulk pack.
  2. "Multi-buy means savings." "3 for $10" is only a deal if the single price is above $3.34 and you'll use all three.
  3. "Name brands are better value on sale." Compare the sale price per unit with the store brand's regular unit price.
Advertisement

Shrinkflation

When manufacturers reduce package size while keeping the price, the sticker price hides an increase. Unit price exposes it immediately. Keep a note of the unit prices for the 10–15 items you buy most often; changes jump out.

Build the habit

  • Compare at least two options for every staple.
  • Check the top and bottom shelves — premium placement is at eye level.
  • Factor in waste: a cheaper bulk pack that spoils is not cheaper.
  • For concentrated products, compare per use (per load, per wash) rather than per volume.

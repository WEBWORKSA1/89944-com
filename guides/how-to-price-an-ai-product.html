How to Price an AI Product (Without Losing Money on Power Users)

Token economics, gross-margin targets, and the five pricing models that work for AI apps and APIs — with a worked example.

By 89944 Price Lab Editorial · Updated November 2026

On this page
  1. Step 1: Know your cost per request
  2. Step 2: Look at the distribution, not the average
  3. Step 3: Set a gross-margin target
  4. Step 4: Pick a pricing model
  5. Step 5: Engineer the cost down
  6. Worked example

Traditional software has near-zero marginal cost: one more user barely changes your bill. AI products are different. Every request calls a model, and every model call costs money. Price an AI product like classic SaaS and your heaviest users can turn your best customers into your biggest losses.

Step 1: Know your cost per request

Model APIs typically bill per token — roughly a word fragment — with separate rates for input and output. Your real input includes far more than the user's message: the system prompt, conversation history, retrieved documents and tool results are all sent with every call. Measure actual token counts from logs rather than estimating from the visible text.

Cost per request = (input tokens × input rate + output tokens × output rate) ÷ 1,000,000

Step 2: Look at the distribution, not the average

AI usage is usually heavily skewed: a small share of users generates a large share of requests. Plot cost per user per month and look at the 90th and 99th percentiles. Your pricing must survive those users, not just the median one.

Step 3: Set a gross-margin target

Buyers and investors judge software businesses on gross margin. Many AI companies aim for 60–80% after model costs. Work backwards: if the 90th-percentile user costs $6 a month in API fees and you target 75% margin, that user's plan needs to be at least $24.

Step 4: Pick a pricing model

  1. Flat seat price with a fair-use cap. Simple for buyers. Add a soft limit and an overage or upgrade path for power users.
  2. Tiered plans by usage. Starter, Pro and Business tiers with increasing request or credit allowances.
  3. Credits. Users prepay for credits that map to your costs. Transparent and margin-safe, but adds friction.
  4. Usage-based (metered). Pay per request, per document or per minute. Fits APIs and developer tools.
  5. Outcome-based. Charge per resolved ticket, qualified lead or completed task. Captures value directly and is increasingly popular for AI agents.

Step 5: Engineer the cost down

  • Route by difficulty: send simple tasks to a smaller, cheaper model.
  • Cache: reuse long, repeated prompts; many providers discount cached input heavily.
  • Trim context: summarise long histories instead of resending them.
  • Cap output length: output tokens are typically the most expensive.
  • Batch: non-urgent jobs can often run at a discount.

Worked example

An AI writing tool averages 1,500 input and 500 output tokens per request. At example mid-tier rates of $3 and $15 per million tokens, each request costs about $0.012. With 5,000 requests a day across 400 paying users, monthly API spend is roughly $1,820, or about $4.56 per user (before any caching discount). At an 80% margin target the plan needs to be about $23 — so a $24 or $29 plan works, a $9 plan doesn't. Plug your own numbers into the calculator to check.

Rule of thumb: cost sets your floor, value sets your ceiling, and usage limits protect the space between them.

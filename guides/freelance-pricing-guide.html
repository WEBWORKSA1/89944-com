Freelance Pricing: From Hourly Rate to Value-Based Fees

Calculate a sustainable hourly floor, then move to project, retainer and value-based pricing with proven packaging tactics.

By 89944 Price Lab Editorial · Updated November 2026

On this page

  2. Step 2: Quote by project
  3. Step 3: Offer three options
  4. Step 4: Add retainers
  5. Step 5: Price on value
  6. Step 6: Raise rates regularly

Freelancers and consultants rarely go out of business because they lack clients. More often, they are busy but under-priced: full calendars, thin bank balances. The fix starts with knowing your true minimum rate, then moving away from hourly billing as soon as you can.

Step 1: Calculate your floor

Start with the income you want to take home. Add business expenses — software, hardware, insurance, accounting, health cover. Gross that up for taxes. Then divide by your billable hours, which are much fewer than your working hours once you account for sales, admin, learning and time off.

Hourly floor = (Income + Expenses) ÷ (1 − Tax rate) ÷ Billable hours per year

With 40-hour weeks, six weeks off and 65% billable time, you have about 1,200 billable hours a year — not 2,080.

Step 2: Quote by project

Hourly billing punishes you for getting faster. Project pricing rewards efficiency: estimate the hours, multiply by your rate, add a buffer for scope risk (often 15–25%), and quote a fixed fee with a clear scope.

Advertisement

Step 3: Offer three options

Present a basic, standard and premium package. Most buyers pick the middle option, and the premium anchor makes the standard package look reasonable. Make the differences meaningful: speed, number of revisions, ongoing support, strategy sessions.

Step 4: Add retainers

Retainers smooth cash flow. Offer a monthly block of hours or a defined deliverable set at a small discount in exchange for commitment — for example, 10% off for a three-month minimum.

Step 5: Price on value

When you can estimate the business value of your work — revenue gained, costs saved, risk reduced — price as a fraction of that value. A project worth $100,000 to a client can justify $15,000 regardless of whether it takes you 40 or 80 hours.

Step 6: Raise rates regularly

If you are consistently fully booked, your price is too low. Raise rates for new clients first, then for existing clients with notice.

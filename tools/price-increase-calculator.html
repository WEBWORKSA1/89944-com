Price increases are asymmetric

Because a price increase flows straight to profit per unit, you can usually afford to lose more customers than you'd expect. At a 54% contribution margin, a 10% price increase can absorb roughly a 16% drop in volume before profit falls.

Max volume loss = 1 − (Old contribution ÷ New contribution)

How to raise prices without a revolt

  1. Give notice (30 days is common) and explain what improved.
  2. Grandfather loyal customers for a period, or offer an annual lock-in at the old rate.
  3. Add value alongside the increase — a feature, faster delivery, better support.
  4. Raise for new customers first and measure conversion before touching existing ones.

FAQ

What is price elasticity?

The % change in quantity demanded for a 1% change in price. An elasticity of 1.2 means a 10% price rise is expected to reduce volume by about 12%.

Where do I get my elasticity?

From past price changes, A/B tests, or surveys like Van Westendorp. If unsure, model a range (0.5–2).

Why 20% + 10% isn't 30%

Stacked discounts multiply, they don't add. 20% off and then 10% off leaves you paying 0.8 × 0.9 = 72% of the price — a 28% discount, not 30%. Coupons with a fixed dollar amount are usually applied after percentage discounts, and sales tax is calculated on the discounted price in most jurisdictions.

The seller's hidden cost of a promo

A discount cuts straight into profit per unit, so you must sell disproportionately more to stand still. If you earn $50 per unit and a promotion cuts that to $25, you need twice the units just to earn the same profit. This is why many brands prefer value-adds (free shipping thresholds, bundles, bonus items) over deep percentage cuts.

Extra volume needed = (Old profit per unit ÷ New profit per unit) − 1

Better promo patterns

  • Bundle a high-margin item with a hero product instead of discounting the hero.
  • Use tiered thresholds: "Spend $75, save $10" lifts order value.
  • Limit by time and audience — loyalty members, first orders, cart abandoners.

FAQ

Is tax applied before or after a coupon?

In most U.S. states manufacturer coupons don't reduce the taxable amount but store coupons do. Check your local rules.

What's a safe promo depth?

Run the profit check above. If the required extra volume is more than you can realistically sell, the promo destroys value.

Margin and markup are not the same number

Markup is profit divided by cost. Margin is profit divided by price. A product that costs $40 and sells for $60 has a 50% markup but only a 33.3% margin. Confusing the two is one of the most common — and most expensive — small-business pricing errors, because a "40% markup" quietly delivers only a 28.6% margin.

Margin % = (Price − Cost) ÷ Price × 100
Markup % = (Price − Cost) ÷ Cost × 100
Price from margin = Cost ÷ (1 − Margin)

Which one should you use?

Use margin for planning and reporting: your income statement, investors and lenders think in margin. Use markup on the shop floor when you multiply cost to get a shelf price quickly. Just never mix them in the same conversation.

Use fully-loaded cost

Add inbound freight, packaging, payment processing (often 2.5–3.5%), marketplace fees, returns allowance and fulfilment. A healthy-looking markup on raw product cost can become a loss once these are included.

FAQ

Can margin be more than 100%?

No. Margin is capped below 100% because profit can never exceed the price. Markup has no cap.

What's a good gross margin?

It depends on the model: many software businesses run 70–85%, retail often 25–50%, restaurants commonly 60–70% on food cost but far less after labour.
